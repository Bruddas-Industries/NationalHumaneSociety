How Your $10 Monthly Donation Really Helps Shelter Animals

For client review

This article is copied from the current site as far as the first section, to show the article layout. Further down, the published post promises annual impact reports, personalized updates for monthly donors and the option to designate a gift to a specific fund — none of which the current site offers. Worth confirming before it is carried over.

In 2025, an estimated 5.8 million dogs and cats entered U.S. shelters and rescues—a 2% decrease from the previous year, according to Shelter Animals Count's 2025 Annual Data Report. While that's encouraging progress, the numbers remain staggering: about 4.2 million animals were adopted (a modest 0.7% increase), but roughly 597,000 were euthanized, and 757,000 experienced non-live outcomes overall (a slight 1% decline). The national save rate holds steady at around 82%, with two out of three shelters achieving no-kill status (90%+ live release rate). These figures represent real animals facing uncertainty due to overcrowding, medical emergencies, economic surrenders, and limited shelter resources.

At the National Humane Society, we exist to change that trajectory. We raise donations to directly support animals in crisis—providing food, medical care, transport, and foster resources—while also assisting partner shelters nationwide with funding for supplies, low-cost clinics, emergency responses, and capacity-building programs. In an era of rising veterinary costs and ongoing intake pressures, consistent support makes the biggest difference.

That's why a small, recurring gift—like $10 per month—packs such powerful impact. Less than the cost of a streaming subscription or a couple of coffees, this amount delivers reliable funding that shelters can count on month after month. Below, we break it down transparently: where the money goes, real stories of animals helped, why monthly giving outperforms one-time donations in tough times, and how you can get involved.

Where Exactly Does Your $10 Monthly Donation Go? A Detailed Breakdown

Shelters face a wide range of expenses, from daily basics to life-saving interventions. The National Humane Society stretches every dollar through bulk purchasing, volunteer coordination, and partnerships with local rescues. Here's how a modest $10 monthly gift contributes in practical terms (based on national averages and our program data):

Nutrition and Daily Care. A medium-sized dog requires about 2–3 cups of quality dry food per day, costing roughly $10–$20 per week (depending on brand and portion). Your $10 covers a full week's worth of nutritious food for one medium dog or several cats/kittens. This prevents malnutrition, supports immune health during stressful shelter stays, and keeps animals adoptable longer.

Preventive Veterinary Care. Core vaccines, deworming, flea/tick prevention, and initial exams are essential to stop outbreaks and prepare animals for adoption. These basics cost $50–$150 per animal. Your recurring gift contributes toward preventive packages, reducing the risk of illness spreading in crowded facilities and lowering long-term medical burdens.
